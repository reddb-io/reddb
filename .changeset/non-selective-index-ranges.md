One slow index read no longer stalls every other statement. A B-tree range that matches a sizeable part of its table (more than one row in 32, and more than 64 rows) is now answered by walking the table: each candidate is a separate verified read of its row, about thirty times the cost of the same row in a walk, and the read held the publication fence throughout. On a 56,000-row table with two distinct values in the leading index column, the statement went from 12 s to about 1 s, and a `SELECT 1` issued meanwhile from 4 s to 12 ms. When one index is declined for that reason, the next index that matches the statement is tried before the table is walked, so an overlap test (`opened < $1 AND closed > $2`) is answered by whichever bound is selective. Statements also stopped taking that fence on their way in and out: the list of versioned collections is read once per state of the versioning settings, and a result that finds the fence busy is simply not cached.
